## 1. Purpose

This Intellectual Property Policy establishes the general principles used by **B2C Solution** for the ownership, protection, licensing, transfer, and use of intellectual property.

This policy applies to intellectual property associated with B2C Solution's:

- Software
- Websites
- Applications
- Designs
- Documentation
- Branding
- Templates
- Digital assets
- Internal tools
- Development systems
- Other technology-related work

---

## 2. What Is Intellectual Property?

Intellectual property ("IP") may include, depending on the circumstances:

- Source code
- Object code
- Software architecture
- UI/UX designs
- Graphics
- Logos
- Documentation
- Databases
- Algorithms
- Technical processes
- Templates
- Written content
- Trademarks
- Trade names
- Brand assets
- Proprietary business methods
- Other original creative or technical works

Different types of IP may be protected under different laws and contractual arrangements.

---

## 3. B2C Solution Intellectual Property

Unless otherwise agreed in writing, B2C Solution retains ownership of its pre-existing and independently developed intellectual property.

This may include:

- Reusable code;
- Internal libraries;
- Frameworks;
- Components;
- Templates;
- Development tools;
- Automation systems;
- Internal documentation;
- General technical know-how;
- Reusable design systems;
- Internal processes.

A Client's purchase of a service does not automatically transfer ownership of B2C Solution's pre-existing IP.

---

## 4. Client-Provided Intellectual Property

Clients may provide materials required for a project, including:

- Logos;
- Images;
- Videos;
- Written content;
- Brand guidelines;
- Product information;
- Trademarks;
- Designs;
- Other materials.

The Client is responsible for ensuring that it has the appropriate rights, permissions, and licenses to provide these materials to B2C Solution.

The Client retains ownership of its pre-existing materials unless otherwise agreed.

---

## 5. Project-Specific Deliverables

Ownership of project-specific deliverables will be determined by the applicable:

- Client Service Agreement;
- Statement of Work;
- Quotation;
- Intellectual Property Agreement;
- Other written contract.

The applicable agreement should clearly specify whether the Client receives:

- Full ownership;
- A license;
- A limited-use right;
- A perpetual license;
- An exclusive license;
- Another agreed form of rights.

---

## 6. Source Code

Source-code ownership should be explicitly defined in the applicable project agreement.

Unless otherwise agreed:

- B2C Solution's pre-existing code remains B2C Solution property;
- Third-party code remains subject to its applicable license;
- Open-source code remains subject to its applicable open-source license;
- Client-specific code may be transferred or licensed according to the project agreement.

Payment for development services does not automatically determine source-code ownership unless the contract says so.

---

## 7. Reusable Components

B2C Solution may develop reusable components during a project.

Examples include:

- UI components;
- Utility functions;
- Development frameworks;
- Deployment configurations;
- Internal automation;
- General-purpose code;
- Design patterns;
- Internal tooling.

Unless otherwise agreed, reusable components and B2C Solution's underlying development methods remain the property of B2C Solution.

## 8. Open-Source Software

B2C Solution may use open-source software where appropriate.

Open-source software is governed by its applicable license.

Examples of license families may include:

- MIT;
- Apache License 2.0;
- BSD licenses;
- GPL-family licenses;
- Other open-source licenses.

B2C Solution will not intentionally remove or override applicable license obligations.

Where appropriate, third-party licenses should be documented in the relevant project.

## 10. Trademarks and Branding

The B2C Solution name, logo, brand identity, and associated branding elements may constitute intellectual property.

Unless expressly authorized, third parties should not:

- Claim ownership of B2C Solution branding;
- Register confusingly similar marks;
- Misrepresent affiliation with B2C Solution;
- Modify B2C Solution branding in a misleading manner.

Client trademarks remain the property of their respective owners.

## 11. Portfolio and Publicity Rights

B2C Solution may wish to display completed projects in its portfolio.

Whether a project may be publicly displayed should be determined by the applicable Client agreement.

Where confidentiality restrictions apply, B2C Solution will respect the agreed restrictions.

Client approval may be required for certain uses of confidential or non-public project information.

---

## 12. Confidential Information

Intellectual property and confidential information are not necessarily the same thing.

Confidential information may include:

- Business strategies;
- Unreleased products;
- Private technical information;
- Customer information;
- Credentials;
- Internal documents;
- Non-public financial information.

Confidential information should be protected through appropriate contractual and operational controls, including NDAs where necessary.

---

## 13. Employee and Contractor Contributions

Where B2C Solution engages employees, contractors, freelancers, designers, developers, or other contributors, ownership of work created on behalf of B2C Solution should be addressed through appropriate written agreements.

Such agreements may address:

- IP assignment;
- Licensing;
- Confidentiality;
- Work-for-hire arrangements where applicable;
- Moral rights where applicable;
- Use of third-party materials;
- Return or deletion of company information.

---

## 14. Infringement

B2C Solution does not intentionally authorize the unauthorized use of third-party intellectual property.

If B2C Solution becomes aware of a credible IP infringement concern involving its services or materials, it may investigate and take appropriate action.

Reports should include sufficient information to allow the issue to be evaluated.

---

## 15. Client Responsibility for Infringing Materials

Clients are responsible for ensuring that materials they provide do not knowingly infringe third-party rights.

Where appropriate, Clients may be required to provide evidence of authorization or licensing.

B2C Solution may refuse to use materials where there is a reasonable concern regarding legality, ownership, or authorization.

---

## 16. Assignment and Transfer

Any transfer or assignment of intellectual-property rights should be made through an appropriate written agreement.

The agreement should identify:

- The specific IP;
- The parties;
- The rights being transferred;
- The effective date;
- Any limitations;
- Payment conditions;
- Applicable licenses;
- Any retained rights.

---

## 17. Licensing

Where ownership is not transferred, B2C Solution may provide a license according to the applicable agreement.

A license may specify:

- Scope;
- Duration;
- Territory;
- Permitted use;
- Restrictions;
- Exclusivity;
- Sublicensing rights;
- Modification rights.

No broader license should be assumed merely because a Client receives access to a deliverable.

---

## 18. Protection of Company IP

B2C Solution may use reasonable measures to protect its intellectual property, including:

- Access controls;
- Private repositories;
- Authentication;
- Licensing terms;
- Confidentiality agreements;
- Copyright notices;
- Trademark protection where appropriate;
- Documentation;
- Internal security procedures.

---

## 19. Repository and Code Ownership

The location of code does not necessarily determine legal ownership.

For example, code stored in:

- GitHub;
- GitLab;
- Cloud storage;
- Client infrastructure;
- B2C Solution infrastructure;

may still be owned or licensed according to the applicable agreement.

Repository access should therefore be managed separately from ownership rights.
